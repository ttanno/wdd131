Popular Openings

An opening is just a plan for your first few moves. These three openings are a great place to start: they're easy to learn, lead to solid positions, and are played at every level from beginner to grandmaster. Click "Save" on any opening to add it to your favorites list, which is stored right in your browser.

The Italian Game

Beginner friendly

1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 Nc6 3. Bc4

The Italian Game is one of the oldest recorded openings and still one of the best for new players. White develops the knight and bishop quickly, aims the bishop at the weak f7 square, and fights for the center with the e4 pawn.

  • Develops pieces toward the center in the first three moves.
  • The bishop on c4 eyes the f7 square, a common target early in the game.
  • Leads to open, tactical positions that are great for learning patterns.

The Queen's Gambit

Intermediate

1. d4 d5 2. c4

White offers the c4 pawn to pull Black's d-pawn away from the center. It isn't a true sacrifice: if Black captures on c4, White can usually win the pawn back while getting ahead in development. Whether or not Black accepts, White typically ends up with a strong central presence.

  • Called a "gambit" because White temporarily offers a pawn.
  • Declining the gambit (playing ...e6) is a very solid choice for Black.
  • Leads to rich, strategic middlegames rather than early tactics.

The Sicilian Defense

Advanced

1. e4 c5

The Sicilian is the most popular response to 1.e4 at every serious level of chess. Instead of meeting White's pawn head-on, Black strikes on the opposite side of the board, aiming for unbalanced, fighting positions where both sides have real chances to win.

  • Statistically Black's best-scoring reply to 1.e4.
  • Creates asymmetrical pawn structures that avoid quick draws.
  • Has many named variations, so it rewards deeper study over time.
