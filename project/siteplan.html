Chess Strategy & Learning Hub

Website Planning Document — WDD 131

1. Site Name

Chess Strategy & Learning Hub

I chose this name because it plainly describes what the site delivers: a place to learn openings, sharpen tactics, and get better at chess. I play chess myself and keep learning new ideas, so the name reflects both my own study habits and the goal I want visitors to share — steady, practical improvement rather than gimmicks.

Optional domain availability: chessstrategyhub.com

2. Site Purpose

The site is a learning hub for club-level and casual players who want to understand chess openings and tactical patterns. It provides plain-language explanations of popular openings such as the Italian Game and the Queen's Gambit, breaks down core tactics like forks and pins, and lets visitors save their favorite tips and openings for quick reference later. A contact form gives visitors a way to ask questions or suggest new topics for the site to cover.

3. Scenarios

  • A beginner wants to know: “What's a solid opening for White that doesn't require memorizing a lot of theory?”
  • A player who keeps losing pieces wants to know: “How do I recognize a fork before it costs me material?”
  • A returning visitor wants to know: “Can I save the tips I find useful so I don't have to search for them again next time?”
  • A visitor with a specific question wants to know: “Is there a way to ask about a topic the site doesn't cover yet?”

4. Color Schema

The palette borrows from the chessboard itself — deep navy and warm cream for the "squares," with a gold accent for calls to action and highlights.

  • Deep Navy #1b2a4a
    Header, footer, headings, nav background

  • Cream #f5f0e6
    Page background

  • Charcoal #2b2b2b
    Body text

  • Gold #c9a227
    Links on hover, accents, buttons, borders

5. Typography

Two fonts carry most of the site, with a monospace font reserved for chess move notation such as e4 or Nf3.

Headings — Playfair Display

The Italian Game

Body text — Inter

A pin restricts an opponent's piece from moving because doing so would expose a more valuable piece behind it.

Move notation — Roboto Mono (special use)

1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 Nc6 3. Bc4
